Transport Guide

Whether you’re landing at John Lennon Airport, hopping on a train, catching a bus, or whizzin round the city in your motor, we’ve got you sorted, kid. This guide covers all the ways to get around Liverpool with ease. From top tips on Merseyrail and Northern services, to airport links, tunnel tolls, and bus routes across the city and beyond – you’ll be travel-savvy in no time. No matter how you’re getting here or where you’re off to, we’ll make sure your journey’s sound from start to finish. Proper handy, that.

Driving Around Liverpool

Getting Behind the Wheel in the Pool

Driving in Liverpool’s generally a doddle. Once you get your head around the ring roads, bus lanes, and the fact that everyone here thinks they’re Lewis Hamilton. The roads are well-signposted, and if you've got a sat nav or Google Maps, you’re laughing. But here's a local’s guide to keep you on the straight and narrow – or at least the least potholed route possible.

Main Roads & Routes

  • M62: The big one – this’ll take you straight to and from Manchester. Ideal if you're coming in from the east or heading out that way.
  • M57 & M58: Connect Liverpool to the likes of Knowsley, Kirkby, Skelmersdale, and the North. Handy for avoiding town traffic.
  • A561 (Speke Boulevard): Gets you to John Lennon Airport or around South Liverpool. Just mind the roundabouts – they’ve confused many a driver.
  • The Strand (A5036): Runs right along the waterfront past the Liver Buildings and Albert Dock. Iconic view, but heavy traffic at peak times – especially if there’s a match or event on.
  • Queens Drive (A5058): The inner ring road – great for getting around the city without going right through the centre. Bit of a lifesaver during rush hour, la.

Tunnels to The Wirral

There’s two main tunnels if you’re heading “over the water” to The Wirral:

  • Queensway Tunnel: The older one – built in the 1930s. Bit tighter inside and slightly more moody vibes but still does the job.
  • Kingsway Tunnel: The newer, faster one, ideal for heading to Wallasey or New Brighton. Both tunnels charge a toll, but it’s all contactless now – no messing with change.

Parking in Town

City centre parking isn’t too bad if you know where to go – but it can cost you if you’re not careful. There’s plenty of car parks around Liverpool ONE, Bold Street, Mount Pleasant, and the waterfront. Most take card or have app payment options. If you're tight on cash, try side streets around Georgian Quarter or up near the Uni – just check signs for restrictions or resident zones.

Watch Out for These!

  • Bus lanes: These are EVERYWHERE in town and around Smithdown, Lime Street, and near the Strand. If you go in one at the wrong time – that’s a fine straight through your door, lad.
  • One-way streets: Especially in town – get this wrong and you’ll be going the wrong way down Bold Street with abar 20 people filming you.
  • Matchday madness: If the Reds or Blues are playing, avoid Anfield or Goodison like the plague. Roads are shut, traffic’s chaos, and parking’s a nightmare. You won't be going nowhere for a while.

Driving Tips from a Local

Keep your wits about you, especially at roundabouts (we love a confusing one). Don’t panic if someone beeps – it’s probably just their way of saying “hurry up, lad.” And if someone lets you out, a little wave or hazard flash goes a long way – manners cost nowt, yano.

Want to Avoid Driving?

Honestly la, with all the buses, trains, and even Lime e-scooters flying about, sometimes it's easier to leave the car at home. But if you're keen on having your own wheels, Liverpool's roads’ll get you there – eventually.

Just don’t forget your Scouse soundtrack, a bit of patience, and maybe a brew for the glove box. You’re good to go kidda.

Train Routes

Merseyrail Services

Getting about in Liverpool is a doddle thanks to Merseyrail – our dead reliable (bit of a stretch like) local train service. They’ve recently binned off the old 507s and 508s after a solid 40 years of graft and brought in the shiny new Stadler Class 777s. These bad lads come kitted out with Wi-Fi, USB ports, plug sockets, and air con – so whether you’re commuting or just heading out for a mooch, you’re travelling in style and comfort now, la.

Trains run every 15 minutes between 6am and 8pm on weekdays, and every 30 minutes outside of that (from 5am ‘til midnight). Sundays are a bit more chilled – just 30-minute services between 8am and midnight, so don’t be expecting to jump on one at the crack of dawn after a big night out.

On the Northern Line, you can head northbound to Southport, Ormskirk, and Kirkby Headbolt Lane, or southbound to Hunts Cross – proper handy for connections to places like Wigan, Manchester, and Preston. It’s sound if you fancy escaping the city for a bit.

The Wirral Line takes you under the river to West Kirby, New Brighton, Ellesmere Port, and Chester. From those spots, you can jump on to connecting services that'll take you all over – including North Wales, Warrington, Crewe, and more. Ideal for a little day out or a change of scenery, like.

Whether you’re heading into town, off to the beach, or just riding the loop line for the fun of it, Merseyrail’s got your back. And with trains this swanky, you might not even mind when it gets delayed… well, hopefully, cause it probably will.

Avanti West Coast Services

High-speed trains from Liverpool Lime Street to London Euston, getting you there in just over two hours. Expect comfy seats, plug sockets, Wi-Fi, and a smooth ride to the capital.

Northern Rail

Regional services connecting Liverpool to Manchester, Wigan, Blackpool, Leeds, and more. Great for exploring the North of England without breaking the bank.

TransPennine Express Services

Fast intercity trains linking Liverpool to Manchester Airport, Leeds, York, and Newcastle. Ideal for longer northern journeys or airport connections.

East Midlands Railway

Direct services from Liverpool to Sheffield, Nottingham, and all the way to Norwich. A handy route if you're heading east across the country.

Transport for Wales

Cross-border routes from Liverpool and Chester to Wrexham, Shrewsbury, and Cardiff. The best bet if you’re planning a little trip into Wales.

Bus Routes

If the train’s not heading your way or you’re just after a bit of a scenic route through the city, Liverpool’s bus network is spot on. It’s mainly run by Arriva and Stagecoach, and covers the whole city region like a blanket – from the Docks to Dingle, from Kirkby to Knowsley, and even over the water into Wirral and beyond.

You’ll find buses running from the early hours right into the night – some even 24 hours, especially on weekends. Most services are frequent (except when they’re not – it’s public transport, la), and they’re all contactless now, so no need to scramble for change like it’s 2005. Plus, loads of them come with comfy seats, free Wi-Fi, charging ports, and even next-stop announcements, so you don’t end up miles from where you were meant to get off.

Main City Centre Bus Hubs

  • Queen Square Bus Station – right near St George’s Hall and Lime Street. Perfect for routes heading north and east.
  • Liverpool ONE Bus Station – spot-on for southern routes and airport links. Handy if you’ve just finished shopping and your legs are done in.
  • Sir Thomas Street & Victoria Street stops – serve a load of express routes and cross-city services.

Popular Routes Across the City

  • 10A/10B/10: These are your go-tos for getting to Huyton, Prescot, and St Helens. The 10A is especially frequent – you can’t miss it flying down Kenny every few minutes.
  • 14/14A/14B: Head through Anfield, Breck Road, Croxteth and Kirkby. Proper useful for match days if you’re off to Anfield.
  • 86/86A/86C: The mighty 86s – absolute legends of the south end. Run through Toxteth, Wavertree, Childwall and go all the way to Garston and the Airport. These ones get busy with students too!
  • 75/76: Serve Woolton, Halewood, Gateacre, Belle Vale and Netherley. Great if you’re from the south end or heading out that way.
  • 80/80A: Another solid south end route going to Speke and the Airport via Aigburth and Garston.
  • 53: Runs from Bootle through Crosby, Orrell Park and into town. Boss for getting to the north end.
  • 17: Links Fazakerley Hospital, Walton, Everton and into the city. Solid one if you’re heading to or from Aintree.
  • 18: Handy if you’re going through Old Swan and Broadgreen – goes right into town.
  • 27/27X: These are your “Outer Circle” buses. They go through loads of areas – like a scenic tour of every ring road in the city.
  • 61/62: Go through Walton, Anfield, Clubmoor and West Derby – ideal for a visit to the Zoo or Alder Hey Hospital.
  • 82/82A/82B: Used to be one of the busiest routes in Europe – still a proper useful link between Speke and the city centre through Aigburth and Dingle. The views along the waterfront are boss too.
  • 1/2/X1 (Stagecoach): These head between Liverpool and Chester via Wirral. The X1 is a speedy one if you're heading that way in a rush.
  • 500 (Arriva): The Airport Express – quick route between John Lennon Airport and the city centre. Bit pricier but proper convenient if you’re catching a flight.

Getting Your Bus Tickets

You can pay contactless onboard, or use travel apps like Arriva or Stagecoach to buy day savers, weekly tickets or monthly passes. If you're travelling a lot, get yourself a Merseytravel Solo or Trio ticket – covers trains, buses, and ferries in one go. Dead handy if you’re going all over the shop.

And if you’re unsure what bus you need, try the Merseytravel journey planner or just ask someone at the stop – chances are they’ll know every stop from here to Southport and back.

So whether you're nipping to town, heading to the match, off for a scran, or just want to avoid parking in the city, the bus is always an easy and affordable shout. Go ‘ed, get yourself on board!

Airports

John Lennon Airport – Our Local Runway Legend

Right on our doorstep in Speke, Liverpool John Lennon Airport (LPL) is the main airport serving the city. Named after our very own Beatle legend, it’s just under 8 miles from the city centre – proper handy if you're off on your jollies or nipping over to Europe for a cheeky weekend away.

The airport flies to loads of top destinations across Europe and the UK, including places like Dublin, Amsterdam, Barcelona, Paris, Malaga, and even Belfast. Airlines like easyJet, Ryanair, and Wizz Air all operate from here – so there’s always a flight for a bargain if you time it right, kid.

It’s not a massive airport – but that’s part of the charm. You’re in, through security, and at the bar in no time. No messing about like some of those other airports where you need a day off just to queue. There’s cafés, shops, duty-free bits, and a Wetherspoons if you're after a pre-flight pint or breakfast at 6am – no judgement here.

Getting To John Lennon Airport

  • Bus: Hop on the 500 Airport Express from Liverpool ONE or Queen Square. It’s quick, comfy, and has space for your luggage.
  • Train: Closest station is South Parkway. From there, you can get a short bus or taxi to the terminal. South Parkway’s easy to reach via Merseyrail too.
  • Taxi: Loads of black cabs and private hire firms’ll take you there – or use Uber if you don’t fancy chatting.
  • Driving: Dead simple to get to by car – just off the A561. There’s long-stay and short-stay parking, plus drop-off zones for those dramatic goodbyes.

Manchester Airport – The Big One Down the M62

If you’re heading further afield – like New York, Dubai, or even Australia – you might need to use Manchester Airport (MAN). It’s about 45 minutes to an hour away by car or train, depending on traffic and how the M62’s feeling that day.

Manchester’s a proper big international hub with three terminals, loads of long-haul flights, and airlines like Emirates, Virgin Atlantic, Qatar, and more. It’s great for those longer trips or if John Lennon doesn’t have the flight you’re after.

Getting To Manny Airport

  • Train: Direct services from Liverpool Lime Street to Manchester Airport station run regularly. Takes just over an hour, and it drops you right at the terminal.
  • Car: Straight down the M62 and then follow signs for the airport. Give yourself time – traffic’s a lottery some days.
  • Coach: National Express run services from Liverpool to Manchester Airport too, if you’re trying to save a few quid.

Flying Tip from a Scouser

Make sure you give yourself plenty of time to get there, la. Even the chillest airport day can turn into a mad dash if your bus gets stuck behind a tractor on Speke Road. And always double-check which terminal you’re flying from – no one wants to leg it across Manchester Airport at 6 in the morning.

So whether you’re heading off on holiday, flying home, or just tagging along to wave someone off, Liverpool’s got you sorted. Pack your bags, slap on your Scouse passport (aka a boss accent), and off you go!
